## Why was earth hot? 
1. Primordial heat
	Accretion: heat produced when planetesimals and proto-planets collided and merged
	Core formation: heat released as dense metal segregated into the core which released energy
2. Radioactive decay
	Uranium, thorium, potassium and other unstable isotopes

# Plate Boundries

### Divergent Boundries
Two plates move away from one another generating new crust.

### Convergent Boundries
Colder, denser oceanic plate sinks under the continental plate.
If there are two oceanic or two continenal plates, and the colder/denser one sinks under.

### Transform Boundries
Plates slide past one another.
Ex: San Andreas Fault

# Different types of Earth Materials

Mineral: Naturally occuring crystalline solids (Ex: Garnet)
Glass: Non-crystaline solid (Ex: obsidian)
Sediment: Acumaltion of loose mineral grains withered from preexisting rocks that become cemented togehter.
Magma & Volatiles: Originate deep from within the Earth
Rocks: Coherent, naturall occuring solid that consists of multiple minerals
- Igneous: solidified molten rock
- Sedimentary: cemented debris of preexisting rocks
- Metamorphic: Altered by high pressure and temperture

Metals: high-density, shiny, and conduct electricity (Ex: Copper, Gold)
Organic Chemicals: carbon-containing compoinds from once-living organisms (Ex: Oil, Coal)

# Minerals

What is a mineral?
- Naturally occuring
- Solid substance
- Orderly and repeating crystlaine structure
- Defined, but not set, chemical composition
- Inorganic (No C-H bonds)

How do minerals form?
- As magma cools and crystalizies
- When minerals break down and new ones grow during oxidation

Fun fact: A synthetic diamond is not a mineral because its not naturally occuring.
Water is not a mineral but ice is.
